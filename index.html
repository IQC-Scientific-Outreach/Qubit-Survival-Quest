Your mission

Your precious qubit has been prepared in the state |0⟩. Each turn, you must place one object from your hand in its path.

Some objects measure the qubit and might destroy it. Others transform it into a new state. How long can your qubit survive?

How to play
  1. Pick one of the objects in your hand. Click it, or enter its number (1, 2, 3, or 4 in Split Decks).
  2. Measurements (projectors, polarizers and declarations) always let the qubit through if it is in the same state and block it if it is in an orthogonal state. If the state is a superposition in the basis of the projectors, it will make it through with some probability and collapse to the basis of the measurement.
  3. Gates, rotators and box swaps/flips always let the qubit through, but can change its state.
  4. Mixed Deck: you hold three objects drawn from one deck, and each object the qubit survives adds one to your streak.
  5. Split Decks: you hold two measurements and two gates, in separate docks. Only measurements add to your streak. A played gate leaves an empty slot, and each measurement the qubit survives earns one gate back, so use your gates to set up sure measurements.
  6. Try to beat the odds and beat par!
Object equivalents

The below objects do exactly the same thing to the qubit whether we visualize it as bra-ket or photon polarization, and you can switch between those two at any time without affecting your streak. The Analogy's three boxes match them too, with one exception (*): its basis changes swap two boxes exactly, the way the Hadamard does, while a real quarter-wave plate turns the state only a quarter of the way, so one of the two swapped values comes back flipped. A quest started in the Analogy uses the exact swaps throughout, and can switch between the Analogy and bra-ket (whose basis-change cards then stand for the exact swaps) but not to polarization. A quest started in bra-ket or polarization uses real quarter-wave plates and can't switch to the Analogy. Some objects only appear if Complex Numbers (Three Box in the Analogy) are enabled.

In the Analogy, the qubit is three boxes, one for each way of measuring it: X (red), Y (green) and Z (blue). Only one box is ever known; the other two are random. A declaration such as “Z = 1” survives if it's true, fails if it's false, and is a 50/50 on a random box. If it survives, that box becomes the known one. On the cards, a double arrow means two boxes exchange values, a ring of arrows means a box flips (0 ↔ 1), and a dashed box is unaffected.
